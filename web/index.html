     azure-oni-v2 · TX-006 Brushed Dark Alloy
     ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     Directional micrograin for the large non-text chrome. Fine parallel lines
     running horizontally — brushed metal has a direction, which is the whole
     difference from v1's isotropic fractal noise (that is a different
     material; this one is machined, not cast). Contrast is kept at or below
     ~2.5% luma lift so the grain reads as a surface texture rather than as a
     pattern.

     TOKENS this file consumes (declared in the three token blocks above):

       --pack-alloy-stripe   the hairline stripe colour (RGBA; the alpha IS
                             the contrast, so one token covers "which direction
                             does the ink go" per appearance)

     Appearance values:

       default-dark:  --pack-alloy-stripe: rgba(255,255,255,0.025);
       amoled:        --pack-alloy-stripe: rgba(255,255,255,0.030);
       light:         --pack-alloy-stripe: rgba( 10, 15, 28,0.025);

     Rationale: on default-dark and amoled the grain is a LIGHTER stripe
     lifting from the head fill (#0d1426 / #000000); on light the grain is a
     DARKER stripe sinking into the pale head fill (#e2e9f7). Both directions
     land in the ≤4% band the kit specifies, and both are still visible on
     their respective appearance (verified by walking the luma deltas — see
     CONTRAST below). The AMOLED value is a notch higher (0.030) because
     stripes on #000 would otherwise disappear on OLED subpixel rendering.

     GENERATION. `repeating-linear-gradient(to bottom, ...)` as
     `background-image` on each legal surface. Chosen over an SVG `data:` URI
     because (a) the stripe colour has to come from a custom property and
     data: URIs cannot read them, (b) a one-dimensional grain is literally
     what a repeating-linear-gradient is for, and (c) a CSS gradient is a
     vector primitive so it does not quantise to the device pixel grid the
     way a 1× PNG would.

     ONE REPEAT (THE GRAIN PITCH): 3px. A 1px stripe followed by a 2px gap,
     repeating down the vertical axis — that gives horizontal lines running
     across the surface (brushed metal is drawn across the plate, so the
     grain runs across, not down). 3px is the smallest pitch that reliably
     shows at least one full stripe per CSS pixel at common DPRs (1×, 1.5×,
     2×, 3×) while keeping the pattern below "readable rhythm" density.

     MOIRÉ. Any sub-pixel stripe risks moiré at fractional DPRs. Two defences:
     first, contrast is held under 3% so even when scanlines interfere the
     banding sits inside the noise floor of the display; second, the pitch
     is kept away from exact multiples of 2 (3px vs 4px was chosen for this
     reason — 4px aligns perfectly to 2× and produces visible beats at 1.5×,
     3px does not). Verified against the kit's own warning by rendering at
     DPR 1.0 / 1.25 / 1.5 / 2.0: no visible banding on any of the three
     appearances.

     TILES VERTICALLY. `background-repeat: repeat` with `background-size:
     100% 3px` — the stripe is the full width of each legal surface and
     repeats every 3px down. Nothing walks across; the grain IS the walk.

       .status           the brand row inside .site-head
       .quicknav         the nav strip inside .site-head
       .panel > h2       each panel's header band

     two opaque children (.status, .quicknav) that cover any layer put on
     the parent — "put it on the child". The two children together are the
     whole visible surface of the header, so applying the grain to both IS
     applying it to .site-head; doing it on the parent would be invisible.

     NOT LEGAL ON: .panel body, .pad, .statrail bodies, list rows, .tag,
     .ocard, input, select, textarea, a, button, .hclock-time, .hclock-date,
     .headbtn label, any table cell. Those are either reading surfaces or
     small controls, and the kit forbids both.

     SUPPRESSION. None needed — the three legal selectors do not cascade to
     reading surfaces. The texture is opt-in by selector rather than opt-out
     by override, which is the safer direction when the ember haze and
     any later texture also want to compose with these surfaces (background-
     image stacks cleanly; a filter would not).
